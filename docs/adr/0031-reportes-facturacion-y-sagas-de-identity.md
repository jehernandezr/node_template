# 0031. Reports, billing and identity sagas

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-23
- Related: [ADR 0008](0008-sagas-orquestadas-bitacora-persistente.md), [ADR 0024](0024-persistencia-typeorm-sharding-y-sagas-persistentes.md), [ADR 0030](0030-correo-de-identity-por-notifications.md)

## Context

The design of F5 to F11 defines the `reports` and `billing` services, the `@platform/quotas` and `@platform/metering` libraries, and the `TenantOnboarding` and `DeleteAccount` sagas that identity orchestrates. Building them exposed gaps the design does not close: how a service calls its own operations when its internal transport is HTTP, where the log of each saga lives and how it is found later by its id, how the billing modules are split without circular dependencies, and in which window metered usage is added up.

## Decision

- **Own operations through the process.** With HTTP transport, `InternalApiModule` builds a `LocalFirstTransport`: the operations of the service's own contract go through the process's `InternalRouter`, and those of other services go over HTTP. The identity saga calls `identity.tenants.create.v1` without knowing its own URL and with the same idempotency record as a remote call.
- **Where each saga lives.** `TenantOnboarding` lives in the shard of the new tenant: `POST /v1/tenants` refuses a slug already taken, places the tenant and starts the saga; the `identity.tenants.create.v1` step reserves the slug and creates the row in provisioning, with `identity.tenants.discard.v1` as its reversal. No crash leaves a slug reserved without a saga that frees it, and if another sign-up takes the slug at the same instant the saga ends compensated. Identity records the saga in the directory before starting it: if the process crashes between the two writes, the client never received the id and the entry without an instance answers as a saga that does not exist. `DeleteAccount` lives in the shard of the user's home tenant. Identity records each saga in `identity_sagas` in the catalog (id, type, tenant of the shard and who requested it), and `GET /v1/sagas/{sagaId}` looks it up there and only shows it to whoever requested it. `SagaModule` exports `SagaStore` to read the instance.
- **Account deletion.** `identity.users.erase.v1` leaves the user's row in `Deleted` without a real email or name, revokes their sessions, deletes their password and passkeys, unregisters their devices and removes them from all their tenants (each removal publishes `identity.membership.changed.v1` with `Removed`, so billing stops counting them, and does not require the tenant to keep another owner). It then publishes `identity.user.erased.v1` and finally removes the user's row from `identity_lookup`. Without that row the repeated step answers the same without touching anything.
- **Plan features in identity.** The `identity-billing` consumer stores the features of `billing.entitlements.changed.v1` in `tenants.plan_features` with their `changedAt` in `plan_changed_at`, ignores a change older than the stored one and bumps the tenant's permission version after committing; repeating the same change bumps it again, so a retry after a crash finishes the effect. A tenant without a billing account keeps the features of its type. Suspending and reactivating the subscription suspends and reactivates the tenant.
- **Billing modules.** `plans` (catalog), `usage` (records by key, totals and members), `subscriptions` (plan, periods, proration and dunning), `payments` (provider and attempt log), `invoices` (closing, numbering and charge result), `entitlements` (quota reads and the usage of the period) and `accounts` (opening and closing of the account the saga requests). `accounts` and `entitlements` exist so that no pair of modules imports each other in both directions.
- **Usage per day.** `usage_totals` stores what was metered per tenant, day and metric; a period adds up (API calls) or takes the maximum (storage) of its days. Usage does not depend on the subscription period and a period change does not split totals. Users are not metered per day: the members of today count (`billing_members`, fed by `identity.membership.changed.v1`), which is what limits the quota when one is added.
- **Metering in Valkey.** `@platform/metering` counts in `UsageCounterStore` per one-minute window; `ValkeyUsageCounterStore` stores one hash per window and an index under the `{usage}` tag, so its scripts run in a cluster. `UsageFlushJob` sends the closed windows with one minute of margin and one key `<service>:<tenant>:<metric>:<window>` per record, and forgets the window only once all its tenants were sent. Metrics travel as text up to the job, which validates them against the billing catalog. An increment that reaches a window between its read and its deletion is lost: deleting only what was read would not save it, because the rest would be sent with the same key and billing would discard it as a repeat. For that to happen, a request would have to be counted more than one minute after its window ended.
- **Quotas.** `ApiCallMeteringInterceptor` and `QuotaGuard` act on service accounts: what `@Quota(UsageMetric.ApiCalls)` meters and limits is integration usage, and tasks puts it on its five routes. Identity checks `Users` with `QuotaChecker` when an invitation is accepted; someone who is already a member does not take a new seat. Sign-up with an invitation checks the inviting tenant's seats before creating the account (`InvitationsFacade.assertRoomForNewUser`): with the plan full or billing down, no user is left without a membership, which would make the retry fail with an email already registered. Routes that can answer with the quota declare `BILLING.QUOTA.EXCEEDED`; identity repeats the code in `BillingReference` because its contract cannot import billing's. `BillingQuotaChecker` keeps the limit and the usage of up to 10,000 tenant and metric pairs for 10 s (when full it drops the one that has gone longest without a lookup), answers a tenant without a subscription as having no quota and, if billing does not answer, fails with `PLATFORM.RESILIENCE.CIRCUIT_OPEN` (503, retryable), the platform code for an unavailable dependency. The 403 carries the contract code; the platform's Problem Details body does not publish `metric`, `limit` or `used`, which travel in the internal details of the error.
- **Invoice and charge.** The subscription stores the plan the period opened with (`period_plan_id`): the invoice, charged at closing, carries the price of that plan and adds the proration of the period's changes as an adjustment. `CloseInvoicePeriods` runs on a cron at five minutes past every hour in UTC, not on a 24 h interval from startup, which with back-to-back deployments would never elapse. It closes the due periods (and activates without an invoice the trial that ends, instead of a separate `ExpireTrials` job) and then charges every invoice due for collection: the newly issued ones and the due retries of the declined ones (`next_collection_at`). Between invoices it honours the job's signal, so when its deadline passes it releases the lock without leaving half-done charges to another replica. A payment reactivates the subscription only if the tenant no longer owes another failed invoice, and every change to an invoice first locks the subscription with `FOR UPDATE`: two simultaneous payments of different invoices cannot both read the other as failed and leave the tenant suspended with everything paid.
- **One charge per attempt.** Each attempt carries its number within the invoice, unique in `payment_attempts`, and is recorded as `requested` in its own transaction before the provider is called. Its id travels as the idempotency key. If the process crashes between charging and recording the answer, the next run finds the attempt open: while it is less than ten minutes old it leaves it to whoever requested it, and after that it asks the provider about that key instead of charging again. Two replicas charging the same invoice collide on the unique key and only one calls the provider.
- **Retries of a declined invoice.** After decline number n the invoice is charged again after 1, 3 and 5 days, within the dunning grace period. Repeating the same decline does not count it twice. Once the schedule is exhausted there are no more automatic charges and dunning runs its course.
- **Provider webhook.** `mark-paid` requires the invoice's amount and currency (`BILLING.INVOICE.AMOUNT_MISMATCH` if they differ) and stores the reference on the attempt; `mark-failed` stores the reason. A notice applies to the attempt it refers to, never to the one due when it arrives: with `attemptId` (the id the provider received as idempotency key) it is that attempt, and one that is not of the invoice answers `BILLING.INVOICE.NOT_FOUND`; without it, it is the latest attempt if it is still open or already has that same result, and if there is none a new, already resolved attempt is recorded, that of a charge that did not come from billing. A repeated notice, or the webhook of a decline the job already recorded, does not count a new decline nor move the schedule forward. Without `attemptId` a late notice of an earlier attempt that arrives while the retry is still open would apply to the retry: the adapter of a real provider always sends `attemptId`.
- **A notice that contradicts the ledger is refused.** A resolved attempt never changes, and a notice never resolves an attempt it does not name. If `attemptId` names an attempt already resolved with another result (a late approval of an attempt recorded as declined), the notice answers `BILLING.INVOICE.INVALID_STATE` with the attempt id and both results in the details, and neither that attempt, the retry the job may have reserved nor the invoice change. A notice without `attemptId` that would take the next attempt number when the job has just reserved it, open or resolved with another result, is refused the same way instead of resolving the job's attempt with someone else's result; if a copy of the same notice recorded that number with the same result, it answers with that attempt. Refusing keeps the ledger consistent and leaves the contradiction to be reconciled by hand with the provider; recording it as a new attempt or overwriting the old one would mark the invoice paid with a reference that belongs to a charge the ledger says was declined, and would let the in-flight retry charge again with nothing left to record its answer on.
- **Reactivation after a suspension.** A subscription in dunning keeps its period, because it kept using the plan. A suspended one that pays starts a new period that day with the current plan: the months of suspension were not used and are not invoiced. The quota honours the `allowed: false` that billing answers for a suspended subscription even if it has room.
- **Plan change to another currency or billing interval.** The current period ends the day before with a credit for the days the current plan had left; the daily closing invoices it in its currency (charge of the plan it opened with, accumulated adjustments and usage of those days) and the next period starts on the day of the change with the new plan. If the period started that same day, nothing was used and it starts again with the new plan. A period that already ended and awaits its closing is not adjusted: the new plan applies from the next one.
- **Invoices in the tenant's favour.** The credit of a plan change is the unused part of the charge the period already bills, so with fixed prices the total is not negative. But catalog prices can be edited under the same plan id, and then the charge comes from the new price and the credit from the old one: the total can end up negative. An invoice at zero or below is left paid without a charge, and a negative one is logged with a warning so the credit note is issued by hand. No balance in the tenant's favour carries over to the next period. Versioning the plan rows so this cannot happen is pending.
- **Dunning per tenant.** `AdvanceDunning` suspends each subscription in dunning separately and logs the one that fails; the next run picks it up without stopping the others.
- **Configurable fake charge.** `FakePaymentProvider` answers whatever `BILLING_FAKE_PAYMENT_OUTCOME` says (`approved`, `declined` or `pending`); with `pending` the result arrives through `billing.invoices.mark-paid.v1` or `mark-failed.v1`, as a real provider's webhook would. An invoice at zero is left paid without a charge.
- **Report results.** The `ReportResultStore` port stores, serves and deletes the result of each run; `REPORTS_RESULT_STORE` picks the adapter and today only `row` exists (`RowReportResultStore`, in `report_runs.result`). `ExpireRuns` marks runs as expired and asks the port to delete each result, so an S3 destination can be added without touching the use cases.
- **Invoice backfill.** `billing.invoices.list-changes.v1` pages the invoices of a tenant (newest to oldest, with issue and payment dates) and `BackfillProjections` walks it the same way as the task history. `proj_backfills` keeps one cursor per tenant and source (`tasks` or `invoices`), so each source advances and fails separately. The state progress prevents an invoice copied as issued from undoing a payment that already arrived by event.
- **Projections without rules in the adapter.** The state progress of an invoice (`InvoiceProgress`) and which state counts as a completed task (`TaskCompletion`) are decided in the `projections` domain; the SQL only compares the progress stored in `status_progress` and receives the enum values as parameters. The task backfill takes the date of its last change as the closing date of a completed task.

## Consequences

### Positive

- A service orchestrates its own operations with the same guarantees as remote ones and without extra configuration.
- The state of a saga is looked up by its id without walking shards.
- Metered usage is added up once even if the job or a batch is repeated.
- A provider notice can never attach its result or reference to an attempt other than the one it names.

### Costs

- `identity_sagas` is a directory of identity's own: if another service orchestrates queryable sagas, it will need its own or a platform version.
- Identity now requires the internal URLs of billing, notifications, tasks and reports to start; tasks requires billing's, and reports requires billing's as well as identity's and tasks'.
- A charge the provider leaves in progress is not retried: it waits for `mark-paid` or `mark-failed`.
- An open attempt nobody resolved is resumed only after ten minutes, and a closing can run up to one hour after midnight UTC.
- A notice that contradicts the recorded result of its attempt stays refused until someone reconciles it with the provider by hand.
- A negative invoice caused by an edited price needs a manual credit note.
- A failed invoice that arrives through the backfill does not bring the failure date: billing does not store it.
- A monthly period that starts on the 31st stays anchored to the last day of the shortest month it crosses (31, 28, 28...).
- The invoice of the period cut by a change of currency or billing interval is issued by the daily closing, not at the moment of the change.
- A very late usage increment in a window being emptied can be lost.

## Alternatives considered

- Calling own operations over HTTP with the service's own URL: it requires knowing it before listening and adds a network hop.
- A platform saga directory (`saga_directory`, as the persistence design proposes): it serves every service, but no service other than identity exposes its sagas, and it can be promoted when needed.
- Totals per subscription period: it couples `usage` with `subscriptions` in both directions.
- Keeping a contradicting notice as a warning and applying it to the invoice anyway: the invoice would be paid while its attempt says declined.
